# Accessibility, forgetting, and replay experiment

Status: completed. No tested memory-mechanism change passed the acceptance
gate; the exact replay optimization passed.

## 1. Problem statement

The observed failure is not adequately described as “decay is too large.”
Three different latent problems can produce the same missing recall:

1. **Event segmentation** — the new cue may be assigned to the wrong active
   burst, so contextual evidence is either imported across a topic boundary or
   discarded inside a continuing event.
2. **Storage versus accessibility** — a historical episode may still exist in
   the graph but have no sufficiently strong cue-to-engram path.
3. **Retrieval-induced plasticity** — a retrieved pattern may be strengthened,
   weakened, or left unchanged. Treating every activation as equally positive
   creates self-reinforcing noise; treating every old trace as decay-only makes
   useful stories inaccessible.

The engineering question is therefore:

> Can the engine recover a coherent old storyline from a partial cue while
> allowing unsupported associations to become inaccessible, without changing
> unrelated recall sets or introducing fixed top-k rules?

This is evaluated separately from replay performance.

## 2. Research basis

### 2.1 Event boundaries are latent-state inference

Event Segmentation Theory models an active event representation that remains
stable while observations are predictable and is updated when prediction error
increases. Naturalistic listening experiments additionally find that Bayesian
surprise predicts human event boundaries
([Zacks et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3314399/);
[Antony et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11654724/)).
Latent-cause accounts make the same distinction computationally: prediction
error is evidence that the current hidden cause no longer explains the
observation, not by itself a command to strengthen a memory
([DuBrow et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5766042/)).

Consequence for Akasha: `burst continuation` and `surprise` cannot be two
unrelated post-hoc multipliers. Surprise must be interpreted under competing
event hypotheses:

```text
                       ┌─ Hcontinue: cue generated by active burst
time + current cue ────┤
                       └─ Hboundary: cue generated by another/new event
```

The posterior over these hypotheses owns context admission. A separate
plasticity rule owns graph modification.

### 2.2 Stored memory and accessible memory are different

Silent-engram work distinguishes a stored trace from its natural
retrievability: stronger or artificial cues can reveal a trace that ordinary
cues do not access
([Roy et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29078397/)). Reviews likewise
separate memory availability from cue-dependent accessibility
([Frankland et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6903648/)).

Consequence for Akasha: an ablation that removes forgetting entirely cannot
diagnose an accessibility failure. We must inspect both raw relation state and
effective cue-to-engram paths.

### 2.3 Retrieval-induced learning is non-monotonic

Reactivation can increase accessibility and make a trace modifiable
([Gisquet-Verrier and Riccio](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22904371/)).
However, activation is not evidence that every exposed relation should be
strengthened. The non-monotonic plasticity hypothesis predicts no change for
inactive traces, weakening for moderately activated competitors, and
strengthening for strongly activated traces
([Ritvo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31358438/);
[Detre et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23499722/)). Prediction error is
also a boundary condition for some forms of memory updating rather than a
generic learning-rate knob
([Sinclair and Barense](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31506189/)).

Consequence for Akasha: the experiment must measure activation zones and
competition. It must not add a guessed “reactivation bonus.”

### 2.4 Holistic completion needs a closed associative structure

Partial-cue completion becomes holistic when the elements of an event are
bound into a coherent, closed associative structure. An open temporal chain is
not equivalent to an all-elements episode representation
([Horner et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms8462)).

Consequence for Akasha: read-time weakening changes cannot recover a storyline
that was never jointly encoded. Seed formation during causal replay must be
ablated independently from read accessibility.

## 3. Falsifiable hypotheses

### H1 — Missing old medical context is mainly an encoding/path problem

Prediction:

- removing forgetting alone will not reliably restore the early storyline;
- widening causal seed support during the original replay will create shorter
  episode paths and recover more stages;
- a read-only change on the already-built graph will recover at most the
  outcome-local episode.

Falsifier: removing forgetting restores the full storyline without excessive
unrelated recall.

### H2 — Hard accessibility competition deletes useful old engrams

Prediction:

- a soft accessibility prior preserves old supported basins that the current
  second sparse projection sets exactly to zero;
- it must add coherent episode members, not merely increase count.

Falsifier: soft accessibility only adds unrelated long-tail items or degrades
the other frozen queries.

### H3 — Surprise must not be computed from a burst average

Prediction:

- an average burst representation is contaminated by assistant replies and can
  label exact repeated scheduled prompts as novel;
- a correct event model must compare predictive likelihoods under latent event
  hypotheses and keep content novelty separate from relation learning.

Falsifier: the average-representation method assigns near-zero novelty to exact
repetitions across the real scheduled-task sequence.

The real-data probe falsified the average-representation method, so it is
rejected and will not enter the engine.

### H4 — Returned basin completion is not currently part of learning

Prediction:

- changing only basin-head accessibility changes returned recall items;
- it does not change subsequent `memory_events`, hubs, or relations, because
  `graph.learn()` currently receives the sharp residual-push state rather than
  the basin-completion state;
- therefore repeated successful basin recall cannot by itself close a missing
  long-range episode loop.

Falsifier: an accessibility-only replay changes later graph state.

The first paired replay supports H4: seed-only and seed-plus-soft-access runs
produced identical `memory_events`, while their persisted recall rows differed.
This is a missing feedback path, not evidence that accessibility is irrelevant
to read quality.

## 4. Ablation protocol

All runs use an immutable copy of the production sparse index. Online databases
and processes are out of scope.

| Run | Encoding seed | Accessibility | Forgetting | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 | current | current hard competition | current | frozen baseline |
| A1 | scale-stable sparse projection | current | current | isolate encoding |
| A2 | current | soft prior | current | isolate accessibility |
| A3 | scale-stable sparse projection | soft prior | current | interaction only |
| A4 | current | current | disabled | forgetting upper bound |
| A5 | current | current | current | completion-to-plasticity feedback |

The scale-stable projection is not a larger fixed candidate count. Tail
surprisal naturally grows with corpus size, while sparsemax is scale-sensitive.
The ablation normalizes by the observed positive peak before the same simplex
projection, preserving evidence shape without a top-k threshold.

Every run is evaluated on the same frozen query set. The principal outcome is
set-valued presence, not rank:

- episode stage coverage;
- coherent storyline coverage;
- unrelated additions;
- recall count, with an operational review ceiling of 40;
- kept/added/lost items for every non-target query;
- source and basin provenance.

No mechanism is accepted from the target case alone.

For A5, completion is recurrent activity, never external observation. The
read-before-write order remains unchanged:

```text
cue ─→ sharp fixed point ─→ basin completion ─→ returned memory
                                            │
                                            └─ recurrent coactivation
                                               ─→ Oja competition
                                               ─→ next-event graph
```

This permits already-present non-monotonic competition to strengthen strongly
coactive members and weaken moderately exposed competitors. It does not mark
all returned items as ground truth and does not feed the new edges back into
the same retrieval.

### Preliminary full-replay results

| Run | target recall count | raw early-stage recovery | result |
|---|---:|---|---|
| B0 | 19 | no | baseline |
| A1 seed only | 13 | no | target regressed |
| A2 soft access only | 43 | no | exceeded review ceiling |
| A3 seed + soft access | 34 | no | more outcome/meta context, no early raw story |
| A4 no forgetting | 45 | no | exceeded review ceiling and added noise |
| A5 completion learning | 11 | no | later target and several controls regressed |

The A1 and A3 graph event states are identical even though their readouts
differ. A4 shows that removing forgetting increases accessibility but still
does not create the missing early path. These two results support H1 and H4:
the target defect is not repaired by a global decay change.

A5 confirms that the missing feedback path matters, but falsifies the naive
repair. Feeding every returned basin item directly into the current learning
field improved the original episode-local medical query from 15 to 28 items,
yet the later longitudinal query fell from 19 to 11 and several controls lost
baseline items. Repeatedly retrieved meta-memory became a dominant competitor
instead of closing the raw storyline. Completion therefore needs a
burst-conditioned plasticity hypothesis and an explicit non-monotonic
activation test; “returned means reinforce” is rejected.

## 5. Replay performance contract

Performance changes are accepted only if they preserve the complete logical
state:

```text
same sparse index
      │
      ├── old replay ──→ logical-state hash + every recall row
      │
      └── new replay ──→ logical-state hash + every recall row
                            │
                            └── bidirectional SQL EXCEPT must be empty
```

The first exact optimization reuses the causal `BurstAwareFeaturePool` already
owned by the replay and caches immutable transition distributions during one
prepared retrieval event. It does not reduce seeds, edges, pushes, basins, or
captured provenance.

On a 1,000-turn frozen prefix:

- old: `194.85 s`;
- optimized: `37.65 s`;
- speedup: `5.18×`;
- logical-state SHA-256: identical;
- `13,109` recall rows: identical in both SQL directions.

The full 4,808-turn release gate also passed:

- old: `2,879.95 s`;
- optimized: `428.75 s`;
- speedup: `6.72×`;
- peak RSS: `533,028 KiB` to `430,664 KiB`;
- logical-state SHA-256: identical;
- all 16 persisted logical tables except intentionally different build
  metadata: zero rows in both directions of SQL `EXCEPT`;
- `65,697` recall items and all `4,808` recall runs: exactly identical.

## 6. Decision rule

The next engine version is selected by the smallest mechanism change that:

1. improves stage coverage of the target longitudinal story;
2. keeps all reviewed queries qualitatively useful;
3. stays within the recall-count review ceiling;
4. produces deterministic online/replay parity;
5. has a mechanistic explanation supported by the ablation, not only a better
   scalar score.

If no isolated mechanism satisfies these conditions, the baseline memory
mechanism remains unchanged and only the exact replay optimization is shipped.

That is the outcome of this experiment.

## 7. Next mechanism experiment

The next experiment should not tune decay. It should replace the current
independent burst/surprise heuristics with one causal latent-event model and
then test a burst-conditioned non-monotonic plasticity rule.

### 7.1 Latent event inference

Maintain a posterior over run length or active event cause:

```text
P(active event | time gap, cue under burst, cue under background)
```

Dense and lexical observations enter as empirically calibrated likelihood
ratios, not a weighted sum. Causal historical distributions own calibration.
The model is evaluated by prequential log loss: state at turn `t` predicts the
observed turn `t`, then updates. Future turns are never features.

This follows Bayesian online change-point detection rather than inventing a
fixed burst size
([Adams and MacKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742)).

### 7.2 Separate novelty from plasticity

Keep three different quantities:

1. **event posterior** — whether context belongs to the current latent event;
2. **content prediction error** — whether the new turn adds information not
   already represented;
3. **memory activation** — how strongly each old trace was independently
   supported or only recurrently completed.

They must not be collapsed into one `surprise × burst` scalar.

### 7.3 Non-monotonic update test

Classify activation by evidence provenance, with thresholds learned from the
causal activation distribution:

- independently supported and strongly completed: integration/strengthening;
- completion-only competitor: differentiation/weakening;
- inactive: unchanged.

The assistant response committed in turn `t` becomes part of the burst context
available at `t+1`. Therefore a graph-only recollection that truly helps the
conversation can gain independent contextual support on a later turn without
external labels. Repeated meta-memory that merely recalls itself remains
completion-only and should not receive the same positive update.

Acceptance requires:

- recovery of raw early-stage turns in the later longitudinal query;
- no regression in the original reviewed query set;
- repeated scheduled prompts do not accumulate unrelated associations;
- an `abc + accidental e` injection loses completion mass for `e` across
  later overlapping cues while the recurrent core retains it;
- online and replay event/state hashes remain identical.
